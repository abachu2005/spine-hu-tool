# Methods Blurb (Abstract-Ready)

Vertebral trabecular attenuation was measured from routine CT by an automated Python pipeline in which the only machine-learning component is vertebra localization with the pretrained TotalSegmentator model, and every downstream measurement step is deterministic, physician-auditable image processing. Each segmented vertebra was screened for surgical hardware (attenuation above 2500 HU, with a 10 mm streak buffer that also downgrades adjacent levels), and the vertebral body was then separated from the posterior elements by an adaptive morphological opening before a patient-specific anatomical frame was computed for the vertebra by principal component analysis. A spherical region of interest was anchored at the vertebral-body centroid within the central 60 percent of body height, sized to 5 percent of the body volume up to a maximum radius of 8 mm, and held clear of the cortical shell by a distance-transform margin of at least 3 mm, so that placement is determined by anatomy alone and is therefore reproducible across scans. The median Hounsfield unit of each region was reported with automated quality-control grading and was normalized to a 120 kVp scanner reference using published linear calibration factors (Westerhoff et al.), with the Pickhardt L1 thresholds surfaced as literature context only. All measurements were reviewed, and where necessary adjusted, by a physician in a tri-planar viewer, and each export carried a complete reproducibility record and audit trail. In internal scan-rescan testing, centroid anchoring reduced the between-study difference at L1 from approximately 37 HU under an attenuation-seeking placement to approximately 7 HU.
